### The line to pay attention to

> "Dealer contribution may vary and could affect purchase price."

**This is the tell.** Mazda subsidises the 0%, and the dealer may hold firmer on
price because of it.

**Ask for the out-the-door price with 0%, and again without it.** Then compare
the totals, not the rates. A subsidised rate that costs you a discount is not a
discount.

### Read the deadline correctly

19 days is real pressure, and it is also a sales lever. **Mazda runs 0% often.**
If this window closes, a similar offer will likely return.

Do not let a deadline pick a car. In-stock-only also limits trim and colour.

## The federal loan-interest deduction is worth $0 here

**Confirmed** from the full text of TD 10054, 91 FR 57214, published 8 Sep 2026.

The buyer files **married filing separately** with MAGI around **$200,000+**.

The regulation sets only two thresholds. **§ 1.163-16(h)(2)** reduces the
deduction by $200 for each $1,000 by which MAGI exceeds **$100,000**, "or, in
the case of a joint Federal income tax return, by which the modified adjusted
gross income exceeds $200,000."

An MFS return is not a joint return, so **the $100,000 threshold applies.** The
preamble confirms the residual bracket covers "**all other taxpayers**."

```
Threshold (MFS → residual bracket)          $100,000
MAGI                                        $200,000
Excess                                      $100,000
Increments of $1,000 (portions round up)         100
Reduction = 100 × $200                       $20,000
Cap before phase-out                         $10,000
Deduction = max(0, 10,000 − 20,000)               $0
```

The reduction is **twice the entire cap**. The result is zero at any interest
amount. The cliff sits at **$150,000** of MAGI for an MFS filer. **This buyer is
about $51,000 clear of it.** It is not marginal.

Two points the regulation settles along the way:

- **Filing status does not disqualify anyone.** Treasury "confirm that filing
  status does not impact QPVLI deduction eligibility." The phase-out is what
  kills it, not exclusion.
- **The $10,000 cap is not halved for MFS.** § 1.163-16(h)(1) reads "$10,000
  per Federal tax return regardless of filing status." No $5,000 MFS rule
  exists in the regulation.

### Three consequences

1. **Assembly location no longer has any financial consequence.** US final
   assembly mattered only for this deduction. Judge the CX-50's Alabama plant on
   build quality alone, where the evidence already favours it.
2. **No federal reason remains to finance rather than pay cash.** The 30D clean
   vehicle credit is gone, the 30C charger credit is gone, and this deduction is
   worth zero. Compare APR against cash on pure interest cost, with no tax
   offset. **At a genuine 0% APR, financing still beats cash**, because the money
   keeps its time value — but that is the only reason left.
3. **The filing status destroys it, not the income.** The joint threshold is
   $200,000, which is roughly this income. On a joint return the same person
   could reach a real deduction. **This is a question for a CPA, not for this
   file** — MFS versus MFJ turns on many factors, and the spouse lives in another
   state. On this car alone the sum at stake is modest, roughly $760 to $1,000
   across the loan, because total interest at 5% over 36 months is only $3,182.
   The wider filing-status question may be worth far more, and is out of scope
   here.

**Caveat, stated plainly.** The phrase "married filing separately" never appears
in TD 10054. The conclusion rests on the residual bracket and the preamble's
"all other taxpayers", which is determinate but is not an MFS-specific rule. The
regulation is final and effective 9 Nov 2026, so a later notice or FAQ could
refine it.

## Assembly — Alabama against Japan

**For the CX-50 the choice does not exist.** The CX-50 is built only at Mazda
Toyota Manufacturing in Huntsville, Alabama. There is no Japan-built CX-50. The
**CX-5 is the Japan-built car.** So "Alabama or Japan" really means "CX-50 or
CX-5" — a change of model, not of plant.

**The evidence runs opposite to the concern.** The documented, reproduced defect
sits on the **Japan-built CX-5**: Car and Driver's long-term car still bricks its
infotainment at 10,000 miles. Nothing comparable is documented on the
Alabama-built CX-50.

Mazda's reliability fall was attributed to **new technology**, meaning software
and infotainment. Those are designed centrally, not caused by an assembly line.

Three supporting points:

- Huntsville is a **Mazda-Toyota joint venture**, and also builds the Toyota
  Corolla Cross.
- It has built the CX-50 since **2022**. Both the line and the model are mature.
  Plant and model maturity predict build quality better than country does.
- US plants build many of the most reliable cars sold here. Honda builds in Ohio,
  Toyota in Kentucky, Subaru in Indiana.

**Conclusion: do not treat Alabama assembly as a defect risk.** Plant-level
reliability data was not available, so this rests on the evidence above rather
than on direct measurement.
