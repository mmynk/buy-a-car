## Reliability

This is criterion 1, and it is where the research changed the plan.

**Latest Consumer Reports reliability report** (via Carscoops, 12 Apr 2026):

> "Lexus, Subaru, and Toyota lead again while Tesla rises and Mazda faceplants
> in latest CR reliability report"

Headline: *"Mazda Bet Big On New Tech And Paid For It In The Reliability Rankings"*

So the ranking order relevant to this shortlist is:

1. Lexus, Subaru, Toyota — lead
2. Tesla — improving
3. Mazda — fell sharply

### The 2026 Mazda CX-5 is a first-year redesign, and it is having problems

The 2026 CX-5 is an all-new generation. It is longer and wider. The wheelbase
grew 4.5 inches. Standard AWD. It carries over the 2.5-litre naturally
aspirated engine with a 6-speed automatic.

Two things were removed: **the 2.5-litre turbo is gone**, and there is
**no hybrid until 2027**.

Published problems:

| Date | Source | Headline |
|---|---|---|
| 11 May 2026 | Consumer Reports | First Drive: Roomy Interior But Lacks a Hybrid Version |
| 29 May 2026 | MotorTrend | Uh-Oh? Our Year With the All-New Mazda CX-5 Is Off to a Bumpy Start |
| 05 Jun 2026 | Consumer Reports | **5 Reasons to Avoid the 2026 Mazda CX-5 Now** |
| 23 Jun 2026 | TFLcar | 2026 Mazda CX-5 Review: 3 Steps Forward, 2 Steps Back |
| 12 Aug 2026 | Quartz | 2026 Mazda CX-5 drawbacks uncovered by Consumer Reports testers |
| 23 Aug 2026 | Car and Driver | **10,000 Miles in and We're Still Experiencing Intermittent Bricking of the CX-5's Infotainment** |

The Car and Driver item is the most serious. It is a long-term test car. The
fault persisted for 10,000 miles. It is not a single bad unit.

**Conclusion: the 2026 CX-5 fails criterion 1.** A first-year redesign is the
worst point in a model's life for reliability. This one already has a
reproduced, unresolved defect in a daily-use system.

### The CX-50 is the safer Mazda

The CX-50 has been in production since 2022. The platform is mature.

The **CX-50 Hybrid borrows its powertrain from the Toyota RAV4 Hybrid**
(Car and Driver). That is a proven system.

Trade-off: the CX-50 has a lower, more sloped roof than the CX-5. It is the
tightest car here for rear-seat headroom and cargo. That conflicts with
criterion 4.

### Honda CR-V

The CR-V won a **Car and Driver 2026 10Best** award. Car and Driver notes
"solid build quality" and calls it "a better all-arounder" than the CX-50.

It is not a new generation for 2026, so there is no first-year risk.

### Toyota RAV4

Fully redesigned for 2026 and now **hybrid only**. The non-hybrid engines were
dropped. Hybrid makes up to 236 hp. The PHEV makes up to 324 hp and claims
**50 miles of electric range**.

Car and Driver is lukewarm. Cons listed: "Interior materials feel cheap,
doesn't handle as well as some rivals, standard hybrid has a noisy engine."
Verdict: "has a ways to go to catch up to our favorites in the segment."

Consumer Reports is positive (06 Aug 2026): *"Review: 2026 Toyota RAV4
Impresses With Powertrain, Infotainment, and Safety Advances."*

Note this is also a first-year redesign. The mitigation is that Toyota carried
the hybrid system forward rather than inventing it, and Toyota leads the
reliability ranking.

### Washington-specific ownership costs

These matter because the stated ceiling is an **on-road** number, and because
one of them recurs every year.

| Cost | Notes | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Sales tax | Bellevue / King County. Roughly 10% | **UNVERIFIED** |
| RTA excise tax | Sound Transit district. Charged annually as a percentage of vehicle value | **UNVERIFIED** |
| EV registration surcharge | Washington charges extra for EVs to replace fuel tax | **UNVERIFIED** |

The RTA excise tax is the important one. Bellevue is inside the Sound Transit
district. The tax scales with vehicle value, and it repeats every year. A
$65,000 car therefore costs more to keep on the road every year than a $45,000
car does — permanently, not once.

## The Tesla question, given no home charging

Charging is the deciding factor, not the car.

Available: an office charger at Amazon. Unconfirmed.

Why that is weaker than it looks over a 7–10 year hold:

- It depends on continued employment at that employer.
- It depends on the return-to-office policy staying put.
- It depends on charger availability, which degrades as EV adoption rises.
- It depends on staying in a building that has chargers.

None of those are under the owner's control. A home charger is.

If the charging plan fails, the fallback is Supercharging, which costs several
times the price of home charging and removes most of the EV running-cost
advantage.

**A plug-in hybrid removes this whole risk.** The 2026 RAV4 PHEV claims 50
miles of electric range. Charge at the office and daily driving is electric.
Fail to charge and it is simply a hybrid. There is no scenario where an
uncharged PHEV strands you.

## Why not a BMW X3 or Mercedes GLC

They fit the budget. The X3 starts at $52,650, the GLC at $50,900.

They satisfy "do not skimp." They have better interiors, better seats for long
drives, and better road manners.

They also contradict two of the five stated criteria:

1. **Criterion 1, service cost.** The stated requirement is not paying a large
   bill every few months. German luxury SUVs are the segment where
   out-of-warranty repair and maintenance costs are highest. Consumer Reports
   owner-paid data puts BMW at **$7,800 across years 6 to 10** against Toyota at
   **$3,775** — about **$4,000 more**, arriving exactly when a long owner still
   holds the car. BMW's generous 3-year free maintenance plan hides this until
   year 6.

   **Correction:** an earlier version of this file said neither German brand
   ranks near the top for reliability. That was wrong. **BMW places 5th in
   Consumer Reports reliability, above Mazda at 14th.** Mercedes places 19th.
   The case against the X3 is cost, not reliability.
2. **Criterion 3, resale.** German luxury depreciates faster than Toyota and
   Honda.

There is also the Washington RTA point above: a higher-value car costs more
every year, for as long as it is owned.

Of the two, the **GLC is the lower-risk choice** because it is mature. Car and
Driver notes it "received a full redesign in 2023" and gets only minor updates
for 2026. The X3 is a newer generation.

**The better answer to the same want is Lexus.** Lexus leads the reliability
ranking and holds value well. It delivers the premium feel without breaking
criterion 1. A Lexus NX or RX sits inside this budget. The RX is the larger
car, so it also serves criterion 4.
