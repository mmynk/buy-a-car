## 2. The 2026 Mazda CX-5, in detail

### Consumer Reports: six reasons to avoid it

**Source:** Consumer Reports, 5 Jun 2026, Jon Linkov. Not paywalled.
`https://www.consumerreports.org/cars/suvs/reasons-to-avoid-the-2026-mazda-cx-5-now-a1077933851/`

The article gives **six** reasons, not five. CR bought an S Preferred at about
$37,000. **The CX-5 has no CR Overall Score and no predicted reliability rating**
because testing was incomplete.

1. **Touchscreen-based controls.** Nearly every physical control moved to the
   screen. A tester: "It's one step forward and three steps backward." Climate
   control is "a headache." Seat and wheel heaters need the system to
   initialize, then a "convoluted menu." Steering wheel controls became flat
   pads that are hard to use without looking down.
2. **Fit and finish for the price.** A tester: "This is one of the most
   depressing areas of the vehicle compared to past Mazdas." Hard plastic
   everywhere, even on the top trim. **"Zero padding for the driver's right
   knee."** CR's verdict on the $37,000 trim: "acceptable for the class but not
   for Mazda." CR calls the cost-cutting "overt."
3. **Seat comfort.** Pronounced lumbar support, stiff fabric, almost no
   bolsters. CR advises long test drives.
4. **Engine noise.** Loud on the highway, coarse note. Power is limited, so it
   revs high to pass or climb. "Even the slightest acceleration brings the
   unpleasant sound to life."
5. **Mazda's recent reliability.** Brand inference, since CR has no CX-5 data.
   Mazda3 mostly above average. **CX-50 average.** CX-70 and CX-90 petrol
   **below average**; their PHEVs **well below average**. CR's warning: "an
   all-new infotainment system can pose a big reliability headache."
6. **One powertrain only.** No hybrid. Mazda told CR its own hybrid arrives for
   **model year 2027**. CR suggests waiting.

CR's counterpoints: strong standard safety kit on every trim, the suspension
absorbs impacts better than the old car, and it "handles better than most of its
competitors."

### Car and Driver long-term test

**Source:** Car and Driver, updated 24 Aug 2026, Drew Dorian. A 2026 CX-5
Premium Plus, $41,080 as tested, on a 40,000-mile test.
`https://www.caranddriver.com/reviews/2026-mazda-cx-5-premium-plus-reliability-maintenance/`

**Two separate faults:**

1. **The infotainment display bricks itself**, locking the driver out of
   navigation, radio, **and climate control**. This trim has the larger
   15.6-inch screen.
2. **i-Activsense driver-assistance warnings**, worst on adaptive cruise. When
   the warning appears the radar feature **will not work**.

**Frequency:** intermittent, not constant. Worst recorded case: the touchscreen
**froze for the last three hours of a road trip through Ohio.** The staffer:
"I'm glad we had the A/C running since there was no way to change that for the
remainder of our trip." Staff using the facial-recognition profile reported
**fewer** outages.

**Was it fixed? No.** A dealer applied a software update in month 1. At one
month: "our trip to the service bay does not appear to have solved the CX-5's
issues." By 10,000 miles the fault had changed character, not stopped. **A
second dealer visit was still pending.** C/D: "It appears we may have to wait
for future software updates from Mazda to get it all sorted." **No TSB number
is cited**, and C/D never says the dealer reproduced the fault.

**In the car's favour:**

- **$0 spent** on service, wear, and repair in 10,296 miles and 4 months.
- Interior "free from any signs of premature wear and tear."
- 26 mpg observed against an EPA 26 combined. It met the combined figure.

**Against it:** 0–60 mph in 8.0 sec, and 70–0 mph braking in **173 feet "albeit
with significant brake fade."**

### Where Mazda's problems actually sit

**No CX-5 or CX-50 appears on any 2026 least-reliable list.** The Mazdas that do:

- **Mazda CX-90 PHEV — 4th least reliable car in America**, CR score ~20.
  Problems: battery, electric motor, transmission, steering and suspension,
  brakes. Autoblog, 22 Jul 2026.
- **Mazda CX-90 petrol** — CR 23. USA Today, 18 Dec 2025.
- **Mazda CX-70 and CX-70 PHEV — 32**, among the five least reliable midsize
  SUVs. CleanTechnica, 14 May 2026.

**Consequence: drop the CX-90 PHEV from the shortlist.** It is one of the least
reliable vehicles sold in America.

**Consequence: do not transfer the CX-5's faults to the CX-50.** CR rates the
CX-50 **average**, and Mazda's fall is attributed to the CX-70 and CX-90.

### The Tesla resale guarantee does not apply here

**Sources:** Electrek and Gadget Review, both 14 Jul 2026.

It is a **Guaranteed Future Value** balloon-loan product, launched 10 Jul 2026,
financed by an Australian company, Driva.

**It is Australia only.** Both outlets say so plainly. **It gives a Bellevue
buyer nothing.**

Its terms, if it ever arrives here: annual mileage limits agreed upfront, fair
wear and tear rules for the whole term, rideshare drivers excluded. **The catch
is that no percentage of MSRP is published.** Electrek: "GFV programs are only
as generous as the guaranteed figure, and Tesla controls that number."

Supporting figures from Electrek: the average Model Y lost about **25.5%** of
its value in one year, Jan 2024 to Jan 2025. 2022 Model Y Long Range buyers who
paid $62,000–66,000 faced **$28,000–36,000** losses at two to three years.

**Methodology limits, and one is serious.** RepairPal's model pages define the

**Three findings that change shopping behaviour:**

1. **BMW's free plan is the most generous of the eight, and Mercedes gives
   nothing.** That is exactly why CR's years 1–5 put BMW level with Subaru.
   **Free maintenance defers cost; it does not remove it.**
2. **Toyota cuts ToyotaCare for model year 2027**, from 2 yr / 25,000 mi to
   1 yr / 10,000 mi. **Buying a 2026 keeps the longer plan.**
3. **Mazda includes no free maintenance at all.** Toyota gives 2 years, Honda 1.
   Underlying service prices are similar, so a Mazda costs more out of pocket in
   the first two years.

### Tesla Model Y — the surprise is insurance

- **Insurance: Model Y averages $4,021 per year** for full coverage, against
  $3,011 for an Audi Q5. **That is +$1,010 per year.** Insurify study of over
  235 million rates, via Carscoops, 13 Jun 2026. All five Tesla models rank in
  the ten priciest EVs to insure.
- **NHTSA opened a preliminary investigation covering nearly 1.2 million
  vehicles, including 2021–2023 Model Y**, after 156 complaints of front
  suspension link failure. One complaint reports total failure of the front
  lower lateral links, compliance links, and upper control arms at **29,103
  miles**, paid by the owner. Carscoops, 3 Aug 2026.
- No oil changes, and regen extends pad life. But **tyres wear faster** from
  kerb weight, out-of-warranty **battery replacement runs $5,000–15,000**, and
  2025 collision repair averaged **$6,395 for a BEV against $5,105 for petrol**
  per Mitchell International.
- From UK claim data: the expensive EV repairs are ordinary parts. Suspension
  wishbones average **$1,650**, worst case $5,515. The on-board charger averages
  **$2,890**, worst case $14,000. **The traction battery is not in the top
  five.**
